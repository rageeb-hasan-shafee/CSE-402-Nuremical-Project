Slide 1 • Mathematical Physics & Problem Formulation

Gravitational N-Body Problem & Hamiltonian Invariants

Base Reference: Tailin Zhu (2020), N-body Simulations of the Solar System with CPU-based Parallel Methods, University of Bristol.

⚡ Physical Conservation Laws
  • Hamiltonian Formulation: Governed by $H = T(\mathbf{p}) + V(\mathbf{q})$.
  • 10 Classical Integrals: Conserves Total Energy ($H$), Linear Momentum ($\mathbf{P}$), Center of Mass vector, and Angular Momentum ($\mathbf{L}$).
  • Isolated Invariance: Phase space volume preserved via Liouville's theorem ($d\mathbf{p} \wedge d\mathbf{q} = \text{const}$).
🪐 Gaussian Astronomical Units
  • Length ($\mathbf{r}$): Astronomical Units (AU).
  • Mass ($m$): Solar Masses ($M_\odot$).
  • Time ($t$): Days; Gaussian constant $G \approx 2.959122 \times 10^{-4}\text{ AU}^3 M_\odot^{-1}\text{ day}^{-2}$.
  • Numerical Benefit: Eliminates catastrophic roundoff cancellation inherent to SI units ($10^{-11}$).
🌌 Baseline & Synthetic Scaling
  • Solar System Baseline: 10 physical bodies (Sun, 8 major planets, Moon) integrated from NASA Horizons ephemerides.
  • Synthetic Asteroid Belt: Main-belt particles ($a \in [2.1, 3.3]\text{ AU}$) generated to scale $N$ up to 50,000 bodies.
  • Plummer Softening: Set to $\epsilon = 0.0$ for point-mass celestial dynamics.

Slide 2 • Numerical Symplectic Algorithms

Symplectic vs Non-Symplectic Geometric Integration

Why long-term solar system stability mandates symplectic structure preservation over raw truncation order.

✨ Velocity Verlet (2nd-Order Symplectic)

Exact preserve of phase-space area $d\mathbf{p} \wedge d\mathbf{q}$. Conserves a shadow Hamiltonian $\tilde{H} = H + \mathcal{O}(\Delta t^2)$, ensuring bounded energy oscillations without secular growth.

  • Long-Term Stability: $|\Delta E / E_0| < 10^{-10}$ across thousands of orbital periods.
  • One Acceleration Call: Only one $O(N^2)$ force calculation per time-step.
⚠️ Non-Symplectic Controls (Euler & RK4)

Non-symplectic integrators fail to preserve geometric invariants, producing artificial numerical damping or secular energy growth.

  • Forward Euler (1st-Order): Artificially injects positive energy every step ($\Delta E \propto \Delta t$). Orbits spiral outward catastrophically.
  • Runge-Kutta 4 (Classical): 4th-order local truncation error $\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^5)$ gives high short-term precision, but dissipates energy over orbital timescales, causing planetary orbits to decay into the Sun.
  • Computational Cost: RK4 requires 4 full $O(N^2)$ force evaluations per time step (4× more expensive than Verlet).
🔭 Relativistic 1PN Correction: Mercury's Perihelion Precession

General relativistic curvature introduces a 1st-order Post-Newtonian correction to Newtonian gravity, producing Mercury's famous anomalous advance:

  • 1PN Force Term: $\Delta \mathbf{a} = \frac{G M}{c^2 r^3} \left[ \left( \frac{4GM}{r} - v^2 \right)\mathbf{r} + 4(\mathbf{r} \cdot \mathbf{v})\mathbf{v} \right]$.
  • Verification Gate: Matches Einstein's 1915 analytical prediction within $0.05\%$.

Slide 3 • Hardware Sympathy & Memory Architecture

Memory Locality, SIMD Vectorization & Scheduling

Eliminating CPU/GPU memory bottlenecks through hardware-sympathetic data layouts and chunk dispatching.

⚡ Structure-of-Arrays (SoA)
  • Contiguous Vectors: Coordinates segregated into separate flat arrays (`x[]`, `y[]`, `z[]`, `m[]`).
  • AVX2 / SIMD Throughput: Streams 4 FP64 or 8 FP32 elements per vector instruction with 100% bus utilization.
  • Zero Striding: Eliminates 32-byte cache line striding penalty present in Array-of-Structures (`AoS`).
🛡️ Zero Inner-Loop Allocations
  • Static Pre-Allocation: All coordinate and force buffers allocated once at initialization.
  • Pointer Swapping: Time loop advances strictly via $O(1)$ pointer swaps without OS memory manager locks.
  • TLB Stability: Zero heap calls in inner loop prevents page faults, translation lookaside buffer thrashing, and OS jitter.
⚙️ OpenMP Dynamic Scheduling (P/E Cores)
  • Hybrid Architecture: Intel i5-1340P features 4 fast P-cores (hyperthreaded, 8 threads) and 8 slower E-cores.
  • Static Barrier Stalls: Equal static chunk allocation forces fast P-cores to idle waiting at OpenMP barriers.
  • Work Balancing: `schedule(dynamic, 16)` delivers 1.45× speedup by streaming chunks on demand to available cores.
📐 Newton's 3rd Law Pairwise Halving
  • FLOP Reduction: Computes $\mathbf{F}_{ij}$ once and applies $\mathbf{F}_{ji} = -\mathbf{F}_{ij}$, cutting operations from $N(N-1)$ to $\frac{N(N-1)}{2}$ (50% cut).
  • Race Prevention: Thread-private force accumulation buffers avoid expensive hardware atomic operations.
  • Break-Even Point: At large $N$, 2× arithmetic reduction strictly dominates reduction overhead.

🎯 Supervisor Oral Defense Technical Reference
Q1: Why does OpenMP run SLOWER than serial execution at N = 100? ZHU (2020) FIG. 4 ▾
Root Cause: Thread creation and synchronization overhead (`GOMP_parallel`, barrier synchronization) costs ~2-5 microseconds. For $N=100$, total computational workload is only $100 \times 99 / 2 = 4,950$ FLOPs (~0.03 ms of CPU work). The synchronization latency dominates the runtime. OpenMP parallel speedup only breaks even when $N \ge 500$, as documented in Zhu (2020) Figure 4.
Q2: Why is FP32 28x faster than FP64 on the NVIDIA RTX 5090? CUDA HARDWARE ▾
Hardware Architecture: Consumer GeForce Ada/Blackwell GPUs are physically provisioned with a 1:64 or 1:32 FP64-to-FP32 ALU ratio (only 1 double-precision unit for every 32/64 single-precision CUDA cores). Furthermore, FP32 cuts memory bus bandwidth requirements by exactly 50%, enabling higher warp occupancy and achieving 609.4× speedup at $N=10,000$.
Q3: In MPI, why does `allgather` scale significantly better than `master-worker`? DISTRIBUTED MPI ▾
Communication Complexity: The `master-worker` pattern routes all $N$ particle states through Rank 0 at every time step, creating an $O(P \times N)$ network serialization bottleneck where Rank 0's network/memory bus saturates. In contrast, `MPI_Allgather` utilizes a hardware ring/hypercube collective algorithm ($O(\log P)$ latency and $O(N)$ bandwidth), enabling distributed ranks to exchange coordinates concurrently.
