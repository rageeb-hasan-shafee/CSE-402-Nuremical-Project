Slide 1 • Problem Formulation

Direct All-Pairs Gravitational N-Body Simulation

Base Research Reference: Tailin Zhu (2020), N-body Simulations of the Solar System with CPU-based Parallel Methods, School of Physics, University of Bristol.

$$\mathbf{a}_i = \sum_{j \neq i}^N G \cdot m_j \cdot \frac{\mathbf{r}_j - \mathbf{r}_i}{\left(\|\mathbf{r}_j - \mathbf{r}_i\|^2 + \epsilon^2\right)^{3/2}}$$
  • Physical System: Exact orbital dynamics of the Solar System (Sun, 8 major planets, Moon) scaled to tens of thousands of bodies with synthetic Main Asteroid Belt bodies ($a \in [2.1, 3.3]\text{ AU}$).
  • Gaussian Astronomical Units: Length in $\text{AU}$, time in days, mass in Solar Masses ($M_\odot$), $G \approx 2.9591220828559 \times 10^{-4}\text{ AU}^3 M_\odot^{-1}\text{ day}^{-2}$.
  • Exact Complexity: $O(N^2)$ direct summation without tree approximations (Plummer softening $\epsilon = 0.0$ for point masses).

Slide 2 • Numerical Methods & Relativity

Symplectic Integration & General Relativity Precession

Symplectic Velocity Verlet

$$\mathbf{r}_{k+1} = \mathbf{r}_k + \mathbf{v}_k \Delta t + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}_k \Delta t^2$$ $$\mathbf{v}_{k+1} = \mathbf{v}_k + \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{a}_k + \mathbf{a}_{k+1})\Delta t$$

Preserves phase-space volume (Liouville's theorem). Bounded energy oscillations ($|\Delta E / E_0| < 10^{-7}$) with only 1 force evaluation per step ($4\times$ faster than RK4).

1PN Post-Newtonian GR Correction

$$\Delta \mathbf{a}_{1\text{PN}} = \frac{G M_\odot}{c^2 r^3}\left[\left(\frac{4GM_\odot}{r} - v^2\right)\mathbf{r} + 4(\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}\right]$$

Reproduces Mercury's anomalous perihelion precession: Analytic Einsteinian $= 42.980''/\text{century}$; our simulation measures 42.983''/century ($0.007\%$ error).

Slide 4 • Scientific Benchmark Insights

Key Empirical Findings Across 289 Ingested Runs

  • Zhu (2020) Fig. 4 Replication (Small-$N$ Barrier Latency): At $N=100$, 1 thread takes $0.035\text{ ms}$, while 12 threads take $0.070\text{ ms}$ ($2\times$ slower). Thread creation and barrier synchronization overhead exceed the arithmetic payload; multi-threading must only be engaged for $N \ge 500$.
  • Intel Hybrid P/E vs AMD Zen 3 Scheduling: On Intel i5-1340P (asymmetric 4P+8E cores), `dynamic` schedule gives a $1.45\times$ speedup over `static` by balancing P-cores. On AMD Ryzen 5600G (6 symmetric Zen 3 cores), `static` performs identically with zero chunk-scheduling overhead.
  • Newton's 3rd Law 50% Operation Cut: Enforcing $\mathbf{F}_{ij} = -\mathbf{F}_{ji}$ slashes pairwise calculations from $N(N-1)$ to $\frac{N(N-1)}{2}$, accelerating Intel i5 by $1.8\times$ ($24.48\text{ ms}$ at $N=5,000$).
  • RTX 5090 Shared Memory Caching & FP32 ALU Gain: Shared memory tiling ($B=256$) reduces global VRAM memory traffic by $256\times$. Because consumer GPUs allocate fewer FP64 ALUs, single-precision (`tiled-f32`) runs $28\times$ faster than FP64 ($0.556\text{ ms/step}$ at $N=10,000$, $>600\times$ faster than serial CPU).

🏗️ High-Performance System Architecture

Contract v1 Standard

Structure-of-Arrays (SoA)

Particles are stored in separate contiguous arrays (`x`, `y`, `z`, `m`). This allows CPU and GPU memory controllers to stream 64-byte cache lines directly into vector registers (AVX2 / SIMD) with zero wasted bandwidth.

Zero Dynamic Allocations

All buffers (`pos`, `vel`, `acc`, `temp`) are allocated once during initialization. In the time-stepping loop, buffers are updated in-place or swapped via $O(1)$ pointer swaps to eliminate OS memory manager stalls.

Strict Contract v1 I/O

All backends adhere to the standardized CLI: `--input`, `--steps`, `--dt`, `--workers`, `--variant`, and `--output-json`, enabling unified verification against golden reference states.

🎓 Supervisor Defense Q&A Cheat Sheet

Q1: Why did you use Velocity Verlet instead of Euler or Runge-Kutta 4 (RK4)? NUMERICAL METHODS
Key Defense Answer: Velocity Verlet is a symplectic integrator. Hamiltonian systems preserve phase-space volume (Liouville's theorem). Standard non-symplectic integrators like Euler or RK4 continuously bleed or inject energy into orbital trajectories, causing planets to either spiral into the Sun or fly outward into space over thousands of orbits. Velocity Verlet keeps energy bounded in an exact oscillating band ($|\Delta E / E_0| < 10^{-7}$). Furthermore, Velocity Verlet requires only 1 force evaluation per time step (compared to 4 evaluations in RK4), making it $4\times$ faster while strictly conserving the Hamiltonian.
Q2: Why does OpenMP run SLOWER than single-core serial execution at N = 100? OPENMP LATENCY
Key Defense Answer: This empirical finding replicates Figure 4 of Tailin Zhu (2020). At $N=100$, computing all forces takes only $\approx 0.035\text{ ms}$. The operating system and OpenMP runtime overhead of spawning threads, synchronizing barrier threads at `#pragma omp parallel`, and moving data across CPU caches takes significantly longer than the arithmetic payload. Parallel speedup only manifests once computational workload exceeds synchronization overhead ($N \ge 500$).
Q3: Why does `dynamic` scheduling beat `static` on Intel i5-1340P, but not on AMD Ryzen? MICROARCHITECTURE
Key Defense Answer: Intel's 13th Gen i5-1340P is an asymmetric hybrid architecture with 4 fast Performance-cores and 8 slower Efficient-cores. Under `static` schedule, each thread receives an identical $N/P$ body chunk. The fast P-cores finish early and sit idle at the OpenMP barrier waiting for the slow E-cores to catch up. Under `dynamic` schedule with chunk size 16, P-cores consume chunks faster, balancing the asymmetric cores and achieving a $1.45\times$ speedup. In contrast, the AMD Ryzen 5 5600G has 6 identical symmetric Zen 3 cores, so static work-sharing is inherently balanced with zero chunk-scheduling overhead.
Q4: How does Newton's 3rd Law (`newton3`) cut floating-point operations by 50%? ALGORITHMS
Key Defense Answer: By Newton's third law, the force exerted by body $j$ on body $i$ is equal and opposite ($\mathbf{F}_{ji} = -\mathbf{F}_{ij}$). Instead of looping over all $N \times N$ pairs, `newton3` loops only over $j > i$ ($\approx \frac{N(N-1)}{2}$ interactions), halving the number of distance calculations and square roots. To avoid data races between parallel threads updating the same body, each thread accumulates forces into thread-private buffers which are reduced at the end of the step.
Q5: How does Shared Memory Tiling accelerate CUDA GPU execution? CUDA GPU
Key Defense Answer: In naive CUDA N-body, every thread repeatedly fetches $N$ body coordinates from high-latency global GPU VRAM ($O(N^2)$ global memory transactions). In our `tiled` kernel, threads in a 256-thread block cooperatively load a tile of 256 bodies into ultra-fast on-chip `__shared__` memory (SRAM) with `__syncthreads()`. All 256 threads then calculate forces against this cached tile simultaneously. This cuts global VRAM bandwidth traffic by a factor of $B = 256\times$, eliminating the memory bus bottleneck.
Q6: Why is single-precision (FP32) 28× faster than double-precision (FP64) on RTX 5090? HARDWARE ACCELERATION
Key Defense Answer: Consumer GeForce GPUs (including the RTX 5090) are engineered primarily for graphics, AI, and single-precision workloads, possessing a hardware FP64-to-FP32 ALU ratio of 1:64 or 1:32 (unlike data center chips like the NVIDIA A100/H100 which have 1:2 FP64 ratios). Therefore, `tiled-f32` achieves up to $28\times$ higher throughput than `tiled-f64`, completing 10,000 bodies in just $0.556\text{ ms/step}$!
Q7: In MPI, what is the trade-off between `allgather` and `master-worker`? MPI DISTRIBUTED
Key Defense Answer: In `allgather`, every rank owns $N/P$ bodies, updates their positions, and all ranks exchange their updated slices simultaneously via `MPI_Allgatherv`. In `master-worker`, rank 0 gathers all positions via `MPI_Gatherv` and then broadcasts the full state to all workers via `MPI_Bcast`. For high process counts, `master-worker` suffers from a communication bottleneck at rank 0, whereas `allgather` leverages decentralized parallel network topologies.
